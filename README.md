## What Is USDT?

USDT, also called Tether, is a stablecoin designed to track the value of the U.S. dollar. In normal market conditions, one USDT is intended to trade close to one USD, although the price can move slightly above or below one dollar.

People buy USDT for several practical reasons:

- To trade other cryptocurrencies without converting back to fiat after every transaction.
- To move dollar-denominated value between supported platforms or wallets.
- To use a stablecoin in decentralized finance or Web3 applications.
- To hold a digital asset that generally has less price volatility than Bitcoin or Ethereum.
- To use as a quote currency on crypto markets.

“Stablecoin” does not mean risk-free. USDT still depends on the issuer, the exchange or wallet used to store it, the blockchain network selected for transfers, and the market conditions at the time of purchase. A purchase can also become more expensive because of payment fees, spreads, trading fees, withdrawal fees, or an incorrect network choice.

A market order is simpler but gives you less control over the final execution price. A limit order gives you more price control but may not fill, especially when the limit is too far from the current market.

## How Much Does It Cost to Buy USDT?

There is no single universal price for buying USDT on OKX. Your total cost can include several components:

### The quote or market price

USDT generally trades close to one U.S. dollar, but the actual quote can move slightly. P2P sellers may quote above or below the reference price depending on demand, payment method, liquidity, and order size.

### The spread

Express buying and conversion flows may include the spread directly in the quoted price. A transaction can show no separate trading fee while still producing a received amount that differs from a simple one-to-one USD calculation.

### The trading fee

Order-book trading charges a maker or taker fee based on the filled order. The fee is calculated using the filled amount, not necessarily the estimate shown before execution. Partial fills and changing market prices can affect the final result.

### The payment provider fee

Card or bank payment providers may charge their own fee. OKX’s documentation distinguishes exchange fees from fees charged by banks or payment providers.

### The withdrawal fee

If you move USDT to another wallet, you may pay a withdrawal fee. The amount depends on the selected blockchain network and the platform’s current withdrawal schedule.

Before confirming a purchase, compare the final amount of USDT received instead of looking at one fee line in isolation.

## What to Check Before Sending USDT Elsewhere

The blockchain network matters. USDT can exist on multiple networks, and the receiving wallet must support the same network you select for withdrawal.

For example, sending USDT over one network to an address intended for another network can create a recovery problem. Before withdrawing:

- Confirm the receiving platform supports USDT.
- Confirm the exact network it expects.
- Compare the displayed withdrawal fee.
- Check the minimum withdrawal amount.
- Verify the address character by character.
- Send a small test amount when the transfer is significant.
- Keep the transaction ID after broadcasting.

A cheap network is not automatically the right network. Compatibility comes first.
